Where teams actually stand.

Vendor surveys measure who answered a vendor survey. This measures practitioners who sat down and scored themselves against named capabilities, one at a time, with no sales call attached. Opt-in, aggregate, and published whether or not the numbers are flattering.

What is stored.

Counts, not records. There is no submissions table. Every contribution increments a counter and is then indistinguishable from every other contribution — there is no row that represents a person, a session or an organisation. Nothing to leak, nothing to re-identify, nothing to hand over.

No IP address, user agent, referrer, cookie or identifier is read or written. What arrives is a list of integers between one and five, and a sector you chose from six options. Even complete and unaltered, that describes a team’s maturity and not a person.

The honest cost of that design: because nothing is stored per submission, a result cannot be withdrawn or de-duplicated. Someone who submits twice is counted twice. For a maturity distribution that is an acceptable trade; for anything with consequences attached it would not be, and I would have built it differently.

How to read it — and how not to.

This is self-reported and self-selected. The people who run a practitioner diagnostic on a personal site are not a random sample of industry; they skew toward those who already suspect they have a gap. Expect the distribution to sit lower than a vendor’s survey of the same question, and treat that difference as a property of the sample rather than a finding.

Nothing is published for a diagnostic below 25 runs. A distribution over four responses is noise presented as evidence, and publishing it would be worse than publishing nothing.
