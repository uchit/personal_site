Situation

The AI pilot works. Risk won’t sign it off.

The model does the thing. The demo lands well. And it has been sitting in front of risk, legal or compliance for a quarter with no clear list of what would unblock it — because nobody has translated the obligation into engineering work.

Demo works · legal blocked · no path to production

You’ll recognise this if…

  • The pilot has been “two weeks from production” for three months.
  • Nobody can answer “what happens if it gets this wrong for a customer?” with evidence.
  • There is no eval set — quality is assessed by someone trying it and being impressed.
  • Prompts live in application code and change without review.
  • Risk has asked for “the audit trail” and the honest answer is application logs.

What’s usually actually wrong.

Risk functions are rarely obstructing on principle. They are being asked to accept an obligation they cannot discharge, because the system produces no evidence they can point at later.

The gap is almost always the same handful of controls: an eval set, versioned prompts, input and output guardrails, per-decision traceability, and a named human-oversight mechanism for whatever the model gets wrong. None of those are model work. They are platform work, and they are what turns “trust us” into something a regulator can check.
